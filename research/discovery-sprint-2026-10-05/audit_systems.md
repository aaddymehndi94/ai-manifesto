Audit date: 2026-10-05. Frozen source: `35802ada98a8ba1205667f630a30c6f5739725c4`, peeled tag `aaa-v0.5-baseline`. This is a reconstruction and evidence audit, not a new theory, novelty certification, or current literature review. File references and line numbers below refer to that frozen tree. The audited files have no difference from the frozen SHA in the working tree. No other discovery-sprint audit report was read before finishing this report.

## Finding

The reduction from broad coexistence claims to a bounded authorization benchmark contains justified scientific corrections. It also contains an explicit editorial compression that does not establish the equivalence of the discarded compositions to prior art. The archive supports reopening some old comparisons, while preserving the negative novelty judgments on their generic parts. It does not support asserting that any reopened seed is new.

The strongest factual distinction is between **a known component** and **a source establishing the same composition, outcome, prediction, and intervention**. The original generation-one ledger leaves all 154 records at `family_identified_source_match_pending`, with author/year/source unset; the ledger itself says that missing fields do not certify prior art (`research/novelty-ledger.md:12`; construction in `scripts/compile_research.mjs:44`). An excluded intellectual center is explicitly not an assertion of falsity (`research/rejected-ideas.md:3`). Accordingly, “known network effect” is sufficient to withhold a discovery claim, but not sufficient to conclude that a specified cross-institution exit process or a different intervention has already been resolved.

## Corrections that should be retained

1. Scope is a typed set or predicate, with partial orders and incomparable transitions; a universal scalar cannot identify power, welfare or legitimacy (`debates/round-1-systems.md:510`; `framework/open-problems.md:9`).
2. Integrity, conformance, causal sensitivity and governance are separate outputs (`paper/paper.md:26`, `:422`, `:526`; `framework/audit-specification.md:77`). Source signatures establish the accepted issuer and bytes, not observation truth, issuer independence or consent.
3. A policy contrast is a total effect under a declared comparator. Lineage does not identify individual human counterfactuals; unknown and bounds are admissible (`paper/paper.md:448`, `:470`; `framework/open-problems.md:31`).
4. Agent influence is not harm, dependence is not pathology, a commercial benefit is not malicious intent, and authentic conditional commitment can be useful. Bounded coaching and accessibility are indispensable positive cases (`debates/round-1-systems.md:329`, `:368`, `:567`; `framework/concepts.json:45`; `paper/paper.md:842`).
5. Principal, affected person, delegate and governor can differ. Jurisdiction cannot be inferred from a valid signature or expanded by an endogenous evidence rule (`debates/round-2-systems.md:187`; `paper/paper.md:1019`).
6. Formal permission does not exhaust effective authority. Expertise, agenda control, credentials, exit cost and provider infrastructure can alter control with unchanged tokens (`debates/round-2-systems.md:125`; `paper/paper.md:247`, `:1322`).
7. Review is itself an intervention with costs and opportunity losses; “fresh ratification” cannot be treated as an independent causal reset (`debates/round-2-systems.md:359`; `framework/open-problems.md:119`; `paper/paper.md:1036`).
8. No A-grade priority, theorem, regularity, welfare effect or security exploit follows from the fixture. C01's B-proposed status requires stronger comparisons and evidence (`research/final-novelty-audit.md:119`, `:207`).

## Where publication-oriented compression exceeded a demonstrated defeat

“Publication-oriented” here describes the explicit full-paper/position-paper recommendations and the recorded compression, not an inferred motive. The reviews request protocol-resolution results for their surviving contribution (`debates/round-1-systems.md:306`, `:491`; `debates/round-2-systems.md:467`; `debates/round-3-systems.md:18`). That is a legitimate article scope. It is not an argument that the whole discovery agenda must reduce to security protocol design.

The broader held seeds I027 (buyer/seller competition), I049 (hysteresis), and I060 (multiple preference controllers) are retired by the blanket loop in `scripts/evolve_research.mjs:42` but are not parents of any generation-two mutation in `:6–18`. I134 (shared recovery cliff) parents only I166, which is rejected as a “separate research agenda.” I131 (portability) parents I164, a typed scope contrast; this preserves one permission comparison but not its post-transfer performance objective. These are selection losses visible in the recorded derivation graph, not completed structural novelty tests (`research/prior-art/graph.json`, `derives` edges; `scripts/compile_research.mjs:18`, `:21`, `:22`, `:25`, `:26`; `scripts/evolve_research.mjs:16–18`).

Three consequences follow for this audit:

- The generic mechanisms may rightly remain C/D while an unanswered composition comparison stays open. No test result in the frozen tree shows that I137's cross-institution exit effect, I135's interoperable permission semantics, or I166's shared recovery capacity is equivalent to the cited family on the requested outcome and intervention.
- The protocol narrowing removes variables the systems reviews themselves say matter: utility, outside options, strategic adaptation, institutional capacity, deadline formation, and correlated dependencies. These survive in open problems and prose, but are not executed (`debates/round-1-systems.md:377`, `:399`; `paper/paper.md:929`; `framework/open-problems.md:251`).
- The typed fixture's honest limited result cannot defeat claims about world evolution. It computes one terminal update and never makes subsequent interventions depend on newly gained permissions (`experiments/typed-benchmark.mjs:27–36`). Its demonstrated path is `policy -> evidence -> scope`; it does not execute the closed loop asserted as a candidate in `paper/paper.md:359`.

## Registry and coverage cautions

The final novelty audit is a bounded search with explicit full-text gaps, especially Dixon and adjacent adaptive-delegation work (`research/final-novelty-audit.md:24`, `:70`, `:119`, `:226`). The central S-register explicitly marks S08/S09 full text pending and S10 primary abstract (`research/sources.json:13–15`). Paper F-register verification is a generic field-research note, not per-source replication (`research/paper-sources.json:16`, `:30`). These differences should remain visible when judging prior-art equivalence.

The claim graph is a registry of `cites`, `derives`, `supports` and `qualifies` relations, not a tested structural causal model. An edge from a known source cannot establish that a composition predicts the same thing or requires the same intervention. The paper claim registry correctly labels H05/H06/H07 untested and H08 unexecuted (`framework/paper-claims.json:33`, `:45`, `:57`, `:69`).

A concrete publication inconsistency remains: paper Appendix A says C02 is “C with proposed B extension” (`paper/paper.md:1396`), while the canonical record is plain C and the final audit rejects an independent B claim on present evidence (`framework/concepts.json:16`; `research/final-novelty-audit.md:184`). No baseline file was edited; this is an audit finding for later integration.

## Audit conclusion

Keep the narrow benchmark as an honest existence construction and retain all its limiting distinctions. Treat broad rejected seeds as excluded centers, not falsified mechanisms or proof of comprehensive prior-art absorption. Before any later promotion, require a specific old proposition, an inspected closest composition, and an outcome/prediction/intervention comparison. The frozen record has enough material to reopen those comparisons; it has no result authorizing a new theory or discovery claim.

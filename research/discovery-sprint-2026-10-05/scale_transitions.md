# Seventeen individual-to-system transition probes

Research checkpoint, 2026-10-05. These are candidate non-linear mechanisms, not fitted adoption curves. The requested 1 user, 100 users, 10%, 50% and 90% are **probe levels**, never asserted critical fractions. Market, state and global infrastructure are different institutional couplings, not inevitable chronological stages. Specify each population before comparing percentages.

A personal tool becomes infrastructure here only if counterparties, resource production or institutional recognition adapt so that other people's viable participation depends on it. Search for a financing floor, graph cut, missing prerequisite, resource feasibility boundary or recognized rule transition. Larger numbers alone do not constitute emergence.

## E4:T001 — Care independence becomes an observation-network state

**Population and probe scope.** Recipients using routine automated care in a specified local support network

**Candidate critical condition.** Positive success-to-withdrawal feedback can remove a connected corroboration path; total care success or visiting hours need not reveal the cut.

| Probe | Conditional change | Coupling, limit or measurement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One recipient may gain privacy and prompt care; independent family/clinical witnesses usually remain. Measure what the local intervention actually removes. | Probe, not an asserted threshold |
| 100 | With 100 recipients sharing visiting staff, success-driven hour reductions can selectively remove overlaps. Test topology at fixed total hours rather than infer a macro loss from count. | Probe, not an asserted threshold |
| 10% | Use 10% as a probe for where first witness gaps appear; whether any gap exists depends on which recipients and links adopt. | Probe, not an asserted threshold |
| 50% | At the illustrative middle probe, connected corroboration may persist or split. The candidate threshold is a specific context-sharing cut, not 50% itself. | Probe, not an asserted threshold |
| 90% | A high-share probe may leave rare needs documented mainly through the aid; alternatively shared assistants can improve witness coordination and add independent observation. | Probe, not an asserted threshold |
| market | Provider success can influence the human-observation services suppliers keep viable; no single provider controls the entire witness network. | Probe, not an asserted threshold |
| state | A public entitlement rule using supported independence can make local witness loss an assessment-wide condition; independent reassessment can counteract it. | Probe, not an asserted threshold |
| global | Shared assessment templates can transmit the dependency across jurisdictions, but independent local testimony rules can keep the systems separable. Global uniformity is not assumed. | Probe, not an asserted threshold |

**Measurement.** Independent need-witness overlap and reclaimable-support coverage

**Beneficial branch.** The same network reconfiguration can remove intrusive cross-context surveillance while keeping recipient-chosen independent support.

**Minimal falsifier.** At matched hours and entitlements, bridge removal versus nonbridge removal does not change independent need substantiation.

## E4:SC-T04 — Courteous robot density to inherited access precedence

**Population and probe scope.** 1 and 100 agents, then 10%, 50% and 90% of a potential population N>=10,000, followed by market/state/global institutional probes. These are comparison settings, not asserted thresholds or a necessary historical sequence.

**Candidate critical condition.** Each courteous interaction can be acceptable while aggregate waiting occupation creates temporary accommodation. Repeated accommodation becomes an inherited planning default; once independent route restoration costs exceed continuing accommodation, stopping individual robots no longer restores the earlier access regime.

| Probe | Conditional change | Coupling, limit or measurement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One yielding robot may improve access or impose negligible delay; a precedent need not form. | Observe yielding, waiting footprint and displaced uses. |
| 100 | Dense courteous waiting can exhaust a shared approach even with zero local etiquette violations. | Measure concurrent stationary footprint and access, not collision count. |
| 10% | A clustered installation can induce a temporary route concession before a uniformly dispersed higher-adoption setting. | Compare equal robot counts with clustered and dispersed staging. |
| 50% | Repeated concessions can become routine access expectations; maintenance decisions may stop protecting the old manual alternative. | Record precedence changes, old-route maintenance and dormant users' claims. |
| 90% | If restoring independent access exceeds the practical remedy horizon, stopping many robots leaves inherited redesign in place. | Test physical route usability and restoration delay after simulated stop. |
| market | Standard staging practices across suppliers can reproduce occupation even when customers switch providers. | Measure supplier-independent waiting geometry and recognized access rules. |
| state | Public access rules can preserve manual routes or ratify inherited accommodation; ordinary microgrants alone do not decide legitimacy. | Measure protected independent access and the provenance of changed public defaults. |
| global | Shared infrastructure standards can export staging conventions across cities, but local geometry and access law may prevent the same transition. | Compare matched sites adopting the convention with independently protected routes. |

**Measurement.** Recoverable manual-route capacity, persistent waiting footprint and the cost/time of reversing accommodation after mandates stop.

**Beneficial branch.** The same courteous repetition establishes reliable coordination and accessible shared spaces when staging and redesign preserve human alternatives and dormant needs.

**Minimal falsifier.** Repeated accommodation and documented manual-route decay do not increase reversal cost or persistent access precedence after stopping.

**Related compounds.** E4:M135; E4:M131; E4:M133

## E4:SC-T05 — Proposal output to institutional action reach

**Population and probe scope.** 1 and 100 agents, then 10%, 50% and 90% of a potential population N>=10,000, followed by market/state/global institutional probes. These are comparison settings, not asserted thresholds or a necessary historical sequence.

**Candidate critical condition.** External bridges turn proposals into transactions. Those executions create usage evidence that reallocates infrastructure, expanding what later proposals can execute. A change in institutional acceptance or infrastructure capacity—not a larger local permission set—can produce a discontinuous increase in reach.

| Probe | Conditional change | Coupling, limit or measurement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One proposal can become an action when one counterparty has an execution bridge; without a bridge it remains advice. | Trace a proposal to a verified external state change. |
| 100 | Repeated execution may produce a stable demand signal while local proposal permissions remain fixed. | Compare accepted proposal outputs and external transactions under the same interface. |
| 10% | If accepted outputs are concentrated on one route or service, planners may cross an allocation threshold at this probe or another level. | Identify the actual planning decision rule and its observed input. |
| 50% | Allocation toward adapted use can increase executable reach and make independent channels less convenient, feeding further execution. | Measure before/after physical capacity and availability of non-bridge routes. |
| 90% | If external institutions now treat the output as routine, the same local proposal can affect a wider set of consequences than at entry; this is not automatically illegitimate. | Hold local scope constant and measure the reachable external outcome set. |
| market | Competing providers may share external execution institutions, so local switching changes authorship but not the expanded action channel. | Compare provider-specific scope with common counterparty execution defaults. |
| state | Official planning or acceptance can enlarge or limit proposal consequence reach; provenance of the public decision distinguishes authorized collective adaptation from drift. | Record allocation decisions, accountability and explicit public authorization separately. |
| global | Interoperable execution bridges can extend reach across jurisdictions, but absent acceptance or physical capacity breaks propagation. | Test accepted cross-border transformations and the external resources actually reachable. |

**Measurement.** External consequence reach of an unchanged proposal interface: resources, decisions or places reachable through accepted counterparty execution rules.

**Beneficial branch.** The same execution-and-planning feedback builds useful infrastructure where accepted proposals reveal legitimate unmet needs and public allocation preserves meaningful independent channels.

**Minimal falsifier.** Verified bridge execution and responsive planning change no external reachable outcomes under the fixed local interface.

**Related compounds.** E4:M149; E4:M140; E4:M128

## No inevitability claim

Public alternatives, retained skills, independent recognition, protected review capacity, interoperable obligations and legitimately chosen collective rules can prevent or reverse these transitions. A threshold imposed by a toy cost function is not an empirically discovered social phase transition. Estimate gains and burdens for affected nonusers as well as subscribers.

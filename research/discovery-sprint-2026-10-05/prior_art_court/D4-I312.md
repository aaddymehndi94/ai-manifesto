# D4:I312: Separate contestable quality from contestable trial access.

Research-only; PRIOR_ART_CHALLENGED_LIMITED_INSPECTION.

## Proposition

If competing providers can demonstrate quality only after users grant scarce exclusive integrations, competition can shift toward securing early integrations rather than improving demonstrated service, even with free profile export.

## Strongest interpretation

If demonstrating agent quality requires scarce exclusive integrations, provider competition can concentrate audition opportunities even with free data portability. Nonexclusive trials can alter quality investment without changing switching price.

## Five nearest inspected comparators

### D4:S022: [Naked Exclusion](https://rasmusen.org/published/Rasmusen_91.AER.exclusion.htm)

Eric B. Rasmusen, J. Mark Ramseyer, John S. Wiley Jr. (1991). Prior proposition, paraphrased: Rational individual exclusive contracts can jointly prevent entry under scale economies and buyer coordination failure.

Inspection: Author HTML abstract, setup, propositions1–2 and explanation.

- same variables: Yes abstractly: exclusive access, entrants, buyers and scale.
- same causal structure: Exclusive agreements restrict rivals’ viable access.
- same beneficiary: Incumbent; buyers can rationally sign.
- same outcome: Foreclosure, which subsumes blocked auditions under suitable setup.
- same timescale: Two periods with entry.
- same prediction: Individual voluntary agreement can produce collective exclusion.
- same intervention: Collective refusal or restrict exclusionary agreements, subject to actual analysis.

### D4:S023: [Learning and Strategic Pricing](https://cowles.yale.edu/node/145463)

Dirk Bergemann, Juuso Välimäki (1996). Prior proposition, paraphrased: Competition endogenizes experimentation costs; prices allocate learning benefits over time and support efficient Markov-perfect experimentation in the model.

Inspection: Primary Cowles discussion paper abstract; author publication list.

- same variables: Yes partly: competing providers, unknown quality, costly trials.
- same causal structure: Access/prices affect experimentation and learning.
- same beneficiary: Buyer and competing sellers.
- same outcome: Learning and efficient experimentation.
- same timescale: Dynamic stream of purchases.
- same prediction: Quality contestability depends on experimentation conditions.
- same intervention: Allocation/prices that support experimentation.

### D4:S018: [Too Much Data: Prices and Inefficiencies in Data Markets](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26296)

Daron Acemoglu, Ali Makhdoumi, Azarakhsh Malekian, Asuman Ozdaglar (2022). Prior proposition, paraphrased: Correlated information creates externalities that can induce excessive disclosure; platform competition need not fix the inefficiency.

Inspection: Primary working-paper abstract and published-version record.

- same variables: Partly: provider competition and shared resources.
- same causal structure: Individual resource disclosure shapes rivalry and other users’ options.
- same beneficiary: Platforms and users.
- same outcome: Data-market inefficiency rather than exclusive integration.
- same timescale: Market equilibrium.
- same prediction: Competition alone can fail to remove a common constraint.
- same intervention: Mediated access rather than generic competition.

### D4:S003: [Performative Power](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.17232)

Moritz Hardt, Meena Jagadeesan, Celestine Mendler-Dünner (2022). Prior proposition, paraphrased: A firm's capacity to change participant outcomes bounds gains from steering rather than learning alone; the framework also considers simultaneous ex-post optimization by multiple firms.

Inspection: Abstract, definitions, learning-versus-steering decomposition, ex-post optimization and multiple-firm equilibrium section; all proofs not audited.

- same variables: Partly: competition, policy and outside options.
- same causal structure: Outside options and concentration influence steering capacity.
- same beneficiary: Multiple firms and participants.
- same outcome: Performative power rather than audition access.
- same timescale: Equilibrium adaptation.
- same prediction: Provider count is not a complete proxy for constrained alternatives.
- same intervention: Change outside options and feasible policy influence.

### D4:S015: [Competition in Persuasion](https://web.stanford.edu/~gentzkow/research/BayesianComp.pdf)

Matthew Gentzkow, Emir Kamenica (2017). Prior proposition, paraphrased: Competition need not improve information. A condition on feasible experiments determines when more informative outcomes are guaranteed; sequential and costly information introduce further limits.

Inspection: Primary PDF pages1–6: opening counterexample, model, assumptions, Blackwell-connectedness.

- same variables: Partly: feasible experiments differ across providers.
- same causal structure: Limited experiment sets constrain quality revelation.
- same beneficiary: Competing senders.
- same outcome: Information quality rather than formal integration ownership.
- same timescale: Main simultaneous game.
- same prediction: Competition cannot guarantee information under arbitrary information environments.
- same intervention: Expand connected feasible experiments; not just add senders.

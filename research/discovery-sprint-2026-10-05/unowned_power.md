# Unowned power: five attempted constructions

Blue-team phase, 2026-10-05. Working terminology, not a novelty finding.
“Unowned” means that an emergent rule-setting capacity is not represented by a
single corresponding micro-grant and may lack a feasible individual revoker.
It does **not** mean nobody benefits, nobody operates the system, nobody is
legally responsible, or no legitimate public authority could govern it.

## The object to separate

Let `g = (g₁,…,gₙ)` be typed individual grants. Let `z` contain skills, resources,
counterparty recognition, institutional procedures, contractual commitments and
tempo. Let `π` be provider/agent policy within those grants. Then

`z(t+1) = T(z(t), g(t), π(t), counterparties' responses)`.

Formal grants constrain some action classes. They do not fix the transition
function of society. Define effective power only for a specified domain: an
actor's ability to alter the feasible or institutionally recognized choices of
specified affected parties, through a specified control lever and over a specified
time horizon. This is wider than jurisdiction, narrower than all causal influence,
and not automatically illegitimate.

The proposed test is paired worlds with the same typed grant profile and matched
current service outcomes but different `z`. Do affected nonusers have different
recognized options? Can the provider change a consequential rule without needing
new grants? Can revocation restore participation within the needed horizon?

Calling the aggregate `F(g)` “non-additive authority” is insufficient. `F` must
include the adapting institutions and resources. The compound object is
`F(g, z, π, responses)`, not magic hidden in a sum of permissions.

## Construction A: individually granted speed becomes an admission rule

**Components:** I040, I043, I146; D4:I190, D4:I318.

People legitimately delegate routine bidding or administrative responses.
Counterparties increasingly receive prompt machine responses. They shorten
accepted response windows to reduce their own inventory or coordination cost.
Nonusers can no longer deliberate within those windows. Joining an agent service
now becomes privately rational, causing still more prompt responses and further
adaptation.

**Macro power.** A capacity to determine participation tempo emerges, although
no user granted authority to set everyone else's deliberation time. The rule
may be set by counterparties rather than the provider. Provider benefit and
provider control must therefore be measured separately.

**Minimal model.** A counterparty accepts manual response while its revenue
covers fixed cost F. With N participants, manual margin m and adoption x,
manual viability requires `N(1−x)m ≥ F`. Above `x*=1−F/(Nm)`, a channel closes.
This step arises from the assumed cost rule; it is not a fitted social threshold.
An individual can revoke instantly yet remain unable to participate.

**Surprise to investigate.** Reducing individual switching cost to zero might
leave nonuser exclusion unchanged if every replacement uses the same response
standard. Provider competition addresses seller concentration, not necessarily
the missing deliberative channel.

**Positive mirror.** Faster administration can eliminate costly delay and extend
access to people previously unable to attend in person. A public slow channel,
batching or asynchronous deadlines can preserve that benefit without requiring
every participant to move at machine speed.

**Falsifier.** Counterparties do not shorten effective windows as adoption rises,
or a viable unassisted channel persists under the stipulated demand conditions.

## Construction B: successful private settlement changes who can make public objections

**Components:** I003, I054, I149; D4:I016, D4:I129, D4:I249.

Agent settlement resolves routine disputes, reducing complaints. A public or
organizational budget rule funds independent challenge capacity according to
complaint volume. Staffing and practical know-how contract. Later disputes are
harder to file, detect or assemble collectively. Low observed dissent then
supports further reliance on the settlement system.

**Macro power.** The default settlement system increasingly decides which
contested situations become institutionally visible. Individual approval of a
settlement never directly granted the power to determine future appeal capacity.
This can occur without false records, intentional suppression or irrational users.

**Important boundary.** Fewer disputes may reflect genuine justice. Declining
challenge capacity is not itself welfare loss. Test serious unresolved cases,
not raw appeal counts. Representation rules, funding decisions and control of
classification can belong to different actors.

**Paired worlds.** Match settlement success and individual permissions. In one,
appeal capability is demand-funded; in the other, a small independent reserve
is funded regardless of observed complaints. Compare later rare-case hearing
access, collective claim assembly and detection after agent withdrawal.

**Positive mirror.** A shrinking caseload can free resources and spare people
unnecessary adversarial procedures. A preserved reserve may still cost more
than it is worth. The experiment must estimate both burdens and redress.

## Construction C: transaction compatibility becomes constitutional vocabulary

**Components:** I135, I139, I147; D4:I005, D4:I100, D4:I201, D4:I273.

Adapters make heterogeneous commitments executable across institutions. Buyers
reward completed transactions. Conditions expressed in minority moral or local
vocabularies are expensive to preserve and do not affect immediate execution.
Repeated loss of those conditions becomes the practical template. Institutions
later treat that template as precedent. People learn to express claims within
it; successful expression becomes evidence that the template represents them.

**Macro power.** A standard controls what constitutes a recognizable condition
or claimant. Execution rights and the right to contest execution diverge. No
single transaction approval necessarily authorized that representational change.

**Closed loop.** Execution-compatible categories → procurement/reuse → procedural
precedent → claimant adaptation → apparent fit → reuse of those categories.

**Paired worlds.** Identical completed transactions and syntax compatibility;
one adapter preserves defeasibility conditions and claimant sets, the other does
not. Introduce a later contested exception. Compare who can obtain a hearing,
not merely whether the original action executed.

**Positive mirror.** A common vocabulary can make excluded claims legible across
institutions. A standing-preserving adapter may widen, rather than narrow,
effective recognition. Distinct cultures need not adopt one internal ontology.

**Falsifier.** The omitted categories are reliably recoverable in later dispute
and do not change recognized standing or defeasibility.

## Construction D: separate promises consume the same collective veto

**Components:** I049, I053, I134, I166; D4:I136, D4:I145, D4:I246.

Each service offers a legitimate cancellation and a credible local fallback.
Those fallbacks rely on the same caregivers, experts, replacement issuers or
manual slots. Each isolated exit test succeeds. Adoption changes practice and
funding so that shared substitute capacity shrinks. The population can no longer
exercise its nominal vetoes together within the relevant horizon.

**Macro power.** Continuation of a provider ecosystem becomes a practical default
despite universal token revocability. A common provider is not required: diverse
providers can share the same human fallback. Conversely, a shared coordinator
can reduce risk if it leaves durable negatively correlated fallback demands.

**Measure.** Define a joint refusal feasibility set, conditional on essential
services, time, budgets and legally available substitutes. Record which coalitions
can refuse together while preserving specified minimal service, not a single
scalar “autonomy score.”

**Beyond ordinary shortage.** The candidate must explain how individually useful
delegation changes this feasibility set and who can coordinate restoration.
Fixed scarce capacity alone is an inherited congestion problem.

**Positive mirror.** Concentrated delegation can pool high-quality care, expand
accessibility and free human reserves. If those savings fund independent fallback,
joint veto capacity can rise rather than fall.

## Construction E: evidence of superiority contains the cost of previous delegation

**Components:** I002, I023, I046, I049, I051, I165; D4:I003, D4:I183, D4:I288.

Reliable assistance displaces routine practice and apprenticeship production.
The institution compares future agents against its now-weaker human fallback.
That comparison genuinely favors delegation. More delegation then weakens the
future comparison class. Market selection can remove training organizations
before any surviving firm deliberately cuts apprenticeship.

**Macro power.** The ecosystem can become a necessary supplier because earlier
delegation changed the production of alternatives. Continued adoption can be
rational and honestly justified in the actual world. A counterfactual low-adoption
world might nevertheless contain better outside options; it might also contain
worse services. The direction of welfare is not stipulated.

**Critical distinction.** Skill decay and hysteresis alone are not the proposed
center. The compound connects the production of competitors, renewal evidence,
and institutional authority allocation. Its future research test must compare
that closed graph against skill-decay models that already include these effects.

**Paired worlds.** Match current agent quality, grant profile and present expert
stock. Vary whether intermediate-case learning and independent explanation
production survive. Measure future cohorts, renewal decisions and recovery time.

**Positive mirror.** Agents can expose difficult boundary cases, credit expert
explanations and build transferable skill. Better delegation could improve its
own outside options, producing a self-limiting rather than self-necessitating loop.

## Who is the principal?

There need not be one. Distinguish five possible functions:

1. People who granted particular task mandates.
2. People affected by the aggregate admission or representation rule.
3. Actors operating the relevant infrastructure.
4. Actors legally entitled to set a public rule or allocate collective resources.
5. Actors able to coordinate a feasible restoration.

These sets can differ. “The user is in control” addresses the first set only.
“Nobody authorized it” overlooks representative legislation, customary standing
or existing institutional mandates. “The provider owns it” may confuse a
beneficiary with the actor controlling the counterparty rule. Audit all five.

## A stronger reversibility question

Let `R_H(z)` be the set of essential service configurations achievable within
horizon H by legally permitted coalitions, with specified resource budgets.
Instant token revocation changes g. It need not move z back into `R_H(z_before)`.
Reversibility of a chosen service configuration is a reachability problem;
technical corrigibility is a stop/modify property of an agent. Neither implies
the other without a model of the surrounding resources and institutions.

A perfectly corrigible AI can participate in a practically hard-to-reverse
society in constructions A–E. It does not follow that society is literally
non-corrigible forever, that the change is bad, or that the agent resisted.
Preserving an intervention path may be preferable to recreating the old world.

## Development question, not conclusion

The possible shared object is **authority allocation inside an environment that
delegation itself transforms**. Its state includes alternatives, recognition,
evidence production and coordination rights. The discovery target is a
distinguishing prediction or intervention that is lost when these are modeled
separately. No court has yet established that existing work misses this composition.

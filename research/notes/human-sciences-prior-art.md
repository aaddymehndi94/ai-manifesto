# Human-sciences prior-art map

Status: `WORKING NOTE / NOT A FRAMEWORK`

Evidence cutoff: 2026-10-04

Scope: autonomy, paternalism, authenticity, identity, technological mediation,
self-determination, competence, dependency, preference construction, social and
economic power, cultural variation, and religious variation.

This note maps claims that a human–artificial coexistence framework is most
likely to rediscover accidentally. It is not a claim of exhaustive coverage and
does not select a thesis. Sources below are original publications, publisher or
author copies, official institutional documents, and a small number of
authoritative reference works used to orient contested fields. Work dated 2026
is included only when a publisher record or manuscript was inspectable by the
cutoff. Preprints are labelled as such.

## 1. The map in one page

Several conclusions survive across otherwise incompatible traditions.

1. **A choice is not made autonomous merely by being accepted.** Frankfurtian
   endorsement, procedural independence, Raz's adequate-option condition,
   relational autonomy, adaptive-preference theory, and manipulation research
   all deny that observed compliance settles whether a desire or decision is
   one's own.
2. **Assistance changes the assisted person.** Self-determination theory,
   self-efficacy, cognitive-offloading research, automation research,
   apprenticeship theory, and technological-mediation theory give different
   mechanisms by which tools alter competence, motivation, perception, and the
   field of possible action.
3. **Preferences are often outputs of a process, not fixed inputs to it.** The
   construction-of-preference literature shows elicitation effects; endogenous-
   preference economics shows institutions shaping tastes and dispositions;
   recommender experiments show predictions anchoring later evaluations.
4. **Dependence is a power relation, even without coercion.** Emerson's
   power-dependence theory, Hirschman's exit/voice model, relational autonomy,
   and platform-work studies all locate power in scarce alternatives and costly
   exit, not only in explicit commands.
5. **Agency can be distributed while blame remains concentrated.** Distributed
   cognition and sociotechnical systems research reject the isolated actor;
   automation studies and the “moral crumple zone” show how the nearest human
   can retain nominal responsibility after losing practical control.
6. **There is no culturally neutral default human.** Independent and
   interdependent self-construals vary; autonomy and relatedness are separable
   dimensions; WEIRD samples are frequently outliers; religious traditions
   disagree about intelligence, personhood, authority, and the human good.
7. **The short-run performance of an assisted pair is not the long-run
   capability of the human.** Classical automation evidence and early
   generative-AI studies make this measurement distinction indispensable. The
   available AI-specific longitudinal evidence remains thin.

These are constraints on originality. “Preserve autonomy,” “prevent
dependency,” “keep humans in control,” “protect authentic preferences,” and
“design for human flourishing” are established families of claims. A candidate
centered on one of them must specify a new variable, causal mechanism,
prediction, decision rule, or institutional implication.

## 2. Philosophy: autonomy, paternalism, authenticity, identity

### PA-HS-01 — Higher-order endorsement is not desire satisfaction

- **Exact proposition.** A person can have a first-order desire yet repudiate
  its becoming their effective will; person-level freedom therefore cannot be
  reduced to fulfilling whichever desire currently causes action.
- **Precedent.** Harry Frankfurt distinguishes first-order desires,
  second-order desires, and second-order volitions. The unwilling addict is the
  canonical case.
- **Source.** Frankfurt, “Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person”
  (1971), [DOI 10.2307/2024717](https://doi.org/10.2307/2024717).
- **AI relevance.** An agent that accurately predicts a craving has not shown
  that acting on it serves the person's endorsed will.
- **Novelty trap.** “AI should respect meta-preferences” is a direct extension
  of hierarchical accounts of autonomy, not a new foundation.

### PA-HS-02 — Procedural autonomy requires independence in preference formation

- **Exact proposition.** Autonomy concerns how a person identifies with and
  critically reflects on motivations, not the substantive correctness of the
  resulting choice; manipulation, deception, and impaired reflection can
  invalidate an apparently voluntary choice.
- **Precedent.** Gerald Dworkin's procedural account.
- **Source.** Dworkin, *The Theory and Practice of Autonomy* (1988),
  [DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511625206](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511625206).
- **Novelty trap.** “Preference provenance” is a useful engineering label, but
  preference-formation history is already central to autonomy theory.

### PA-HS-03 — Autonomy needs options, capacities, and non-manipulation

- **Exact proposition.** Living autonomously requires mental capacities, an
  adequate range of valuable options, and independence from coercion and
  manipulation. Formal choice alone is insufficient.
- **Precedent.** Joseph Raz's perfectionist account of autonomy.
- **Source.** Raz, *The Morality of Freedom*, chapter 14 (1986/1988),
  [book DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/0198248075.001.0001) and
  [chapter record](https://academic.oup.com/book/9926/chapter-abstract/157265127).
- **AI relevance.** Personalized menus may raise click-level choice while
  narrowing the socially available option set.
- **Novelty trap.** “Option value after delegation” strongly overlaps Raz
  unless it introduces a distinct temporal or measurable relation.

### PA-HS-04 — Autonomy is socially scaffolded and socially damaged

- **Exact proposition.** Self-trust, self-conception, recognition,
  imagination, memory, and non-oppressive relationships can be constitutive
  conditions of autonomy rather than external aids to an otherwise independent
  chooser.
- **Precedent.** Feminist relational-autonomy scholarship.
- **Source.** Mackenzie and Stoljar (eds.), *Relational Autonomy* (2000),
  [DOI 10.1093/oso/9780195123333.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780195123333.001.0001).
- **AI relevance.** A system can undermine autonomy by eroding self-trust or
  reshaping social recognition even while presenting many options.
- **Novelty trap.** “Relational agency” and “socially constituted autonomy” are
  established terms. Merely adding an artificial relation is an application.

### PA-HS-05 — Paternalism is identified partly by its justificatory structure

- **Exact proposition.** Paternalism is interference with another's agency or
  knowledge, against or without their will, justified by improving their good
  or preventing harm. Its evaluation turns on competence, voluntariness,
  stakes, and whether intervention is soft or hard.
- **Precedent.** Mill's harm principle and the later analytic literature.
- **Sources.** Mill, *On Liberty* (1859),
  [Project Gutenberg text](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/34901); Dworkin,
  [“Paternalism,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/paternalism/)
  (authoritative field map, updated 2020).
- **AI relevance.** Silent refusal, friction, selective disclosure, and choice
  ordering can be paternalistic without a robot physically blocking an act.
- **Novelty trap.** “Beneficial refusal” or “protective friction” must confront
  the mature paternalism literature.

### PA-HS-06 — Nudges preserve formal choice while altering choice architecture

- **Exact proposition.** Institutions can predictably steer behavior through
  defaults and framing while leaving options technically available; calling
  this “libertarian” does not settle manipulation, welfare, or legitimacy.
- **Precedent.** Libertarian paternalism and its critics.
- **Source.** Thaler and Sunstein, “Libertarian Paternalism” (2003),
  [DOI 10.1257/000282803321947001](https://doi.org/10.1257/000282803321947001).
- **AI relevance.** A conversational agent is a continuously adaptive choice
  architect, with more inferential and relational leverage than a static
  default.
- **Novelty trap.** “AI changes decisions while preserving consent” is a nudge
  argument unless the feedback, personalization, or relationship produces a
  demonstrably different phenomenon.

### PA-HS-07 — Authenticity and autonomy can diverge

- **Exact proposition.** Reflective self-government does not guarantee that a
  life expresses the commitments through which a person understands who they
  are; conversely, authenticity cannot be reduced to obedience to an inner
  preference presumed to be pre-social.
- **Precedent.** Existential, communitarian, and analytic debates about
  authenticity.
- **Sources.** Taylor, *The Ethics of Authenticity* (1991/1992), publisher
  [record](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/catalog.php?isbn=9780674987692); Varga and Guignon,
  [“Authenticity,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/authenticity/)
  (authoritative map, revised 2025).
- **AI relevance.** An agent may help execute reflectively chosen goals while
  making a life feel alien or authored elsewhere.
- **Novelty trap.** “Authorship dilution” is close to authenticity and
  self-authorship. Its novelty would have to lie in a distinct causal measure.

### PA-HS-08 — Adaptive preferences defeat naive revealed-preference welfare

- **Exact proposition.** People adjust aspirations and desires to constrained
  circumstances; satisfaction or choice under deprivation can therefore
  understate injustice and capability loss.
- **Precedent.** Elster's “sour grapes,” Sen's capability critique, and
  Nussbaum's account of adaptive preference.
- **Sources.** Elster, *Sour Grapes* (1983),
  [DOI 10.1017/CBO9781139171697](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139171697);
  Sen, *Commodities and Capabilities* (1985),
  [author bibliography](https://sen.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/commodities-and-capabilities);
  Nussbaum, *Women and Human Development* (2000),
  [DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511841286](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511841286).
- **AI relevance.** A dependent user may report high satisfaction precisely
  because aspirations and perceived alternatives have contracted.
- **Novelty trap.** “Preference capture,” if it means adaptation to a narrow
  environment, has powerful prior art.

### PA-HS-09 — Identity can be narrative and temporally extended

- **Exact proposition.** Practical identity is partly constituted by an
  intelligible relation among past, present, and anticipated actions, rather
  than by a momentary bundle of preferences.
- **Precedent.** Narrative identity accounts in philosophy and psychology.
- **Sources.** Schechtman, *The Constitution of Selves* (1996),
  [JSTOR/Cornell record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7591/j.ctv75d3xw);
  McAdams and McLean, “Narrative Identity” (2013),
  [DOI 10.1177/0963721413475622](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721413475622).
- **AI relevance.** A persistent agent that curates memory and explains a
  person's history can alter the story through which future choices make sense.
- **Novelty trap.** “AI as co-author of identity” is already implied by
  narrative and socially scaffolded identity; novelty needs a stronger claim.

### PA-HS-10 — Cognition may extend through reliable external resources

- **Exact proposition.** When an external resource is reliably available,
  automatically endorsed, and functionally integrated into action, it can play
  a role analogous to an internal cognitive process.
- **Precedent.** Active externalism and extended cognition.
- **Sources.** Clark and Chalmers, “The Extended Mind” (1998),
  [DOI 10.1093/analys/58.1.7](https://doi.org/10.1093/analys/58.1.7) and
  [author-hosted text](https://www.consc.net/papers/extended.html); Hutchins,
  *Cognition in the Wild* (1995),
  [MIT Press record](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262581462/cognition-in-the-wild/).
- **AI relevance.** Persistent memory agents may become components of a human
  cognitive system rather than optional tools.
- **Novelty trap.** “Identity externalization” and “cognitive sovereignty” can
  be renamed extended-mind concerns unless they identify ownership, switching,
  or adversarial-control consequences absent from that literature.

## 3. Psychology and behavioral science

### PA-HS-11 — Autonomy, competence, and relatedness are distinct needs

- **Exact proposition.** Environments that support autonomy, competence, and
  relatedness tend to improve internalized motivation and well-being; control
  and need frustration can impair them. Autonomy in this theory means experienced
  volition, not social independence.
- **Precedent.** Self-determination theory (SDT).
- **Source.** Ryan and Deci, “Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of
  Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being” (2000),
  [author PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf),
  [DOI 10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68).
- **AI relevance.** High task success can coexist with reduced volition,
  competence, or human connection. These must be measured separately.
- **Novelty trap.** “Agency retained per unit assistance” overlaps SDT if it is
  only a subjective autonomy or competence score.

### PA-HS-12 — Extrinsic control can crowd out intrinsic motivation

- **Exact proposition.** Some expected tangible rewards and controlling
  contexts reduce subsequent free-choice engagement in intrinsically
  interesting activities, though effects depend on reward type and context.
- **Precedent.** Cognitive evaluation theory and the overjustification
  literature.
- **Sources.** Deci, “Effects of Externally Mediated Rewards on Intrinsic
  Motivation” (1971),
  [DOI 10.1037/h0030644](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0030644); Deci, Koestner, and
  Ryan meta-analysis (1999),
  [DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627).
- **AI relevance.** Gamified agent incentives may improve immediate compliance
  while weakening unprompted future engagement.
- **Novelty trap.** “Assistance crowds out motivation” is not new without an
  AI-specific mechanism or boundary condition.

### PA-HS-13 — Perceived self-efficacy changes action and persistence

- **Exact proposition.** Beliefs about one's capability affect task choice,
  effort, and persistence; mastery experience is a major source of those
  beliefs.
- **Precedent.** Bandura's self-efficacy theory.
- **Source.** Bandura, “Self-efficacy: Toward a Unifying Theory of Behavioral
  Change” (1977),
  [DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.84.2.191](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.2.191).
- **AI relevance.** Seamless substitution can remove mastery experiences even
  when it raises output quality.
- **Novelty trap.** “Confidence decay from delegation” is likely a self-efficacy
  hypothesis, not a new construct.

### PA-HS-14 — Control learning, not passivity alone, matters

- **Exact proposition.** Classic learned-helplessness work connected exposure
  to uncontrollability with later failure to attempt control. Later neuroscience
  revised the simple story: passivity may be a default response to prolonged
  aversive stimulation, while learning control recruits circuitry that inhibits
  it and can generalize.
- **Precedent.** Learned helplessness and its 50-year revision.
- **Sources.** Maier and Seligman (1976),
  [DOI 10.1037/0096-3445.105.1.3](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.105.1.3);
  Maier and Seligman (2016),
  [DOI 10.1037/rev0000033](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000033).
- **AI relevance.** Systems should be studied for whether users learn that
  intervention is causally effective, not casually diagnosed as “helpless.”
- **Novelty trap.** Invoking learned helplessness as a metaphor for convenient
  automation overstates the evidence unless controllability and transfer are
  experimentally manipulated.

### PA-HS-15 — Offloading is metacognitively selected and can help or harm

- **Exact proposition.** People use external action or devices to reduce
  internal processing demand; offloading depends partly on confidence and
  perceived external reliability, and its cognitive consequences are
  task-dependent rather than uniformly degenerative.
- **Precedent.** Cognitive-offloading research.
- **Source.** Risko and Gilbert, “Cognitive Offloading” (2016),
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27542527/),
  [DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002).
- **AI relevance.** Delegation policies interact with metacognitive confidence,
  memory, and transfer, so “more internal memory” is not automatically better.
- **Novelty trap.** “Cognitive debt” and “deskilling” need operational measures
  beyond amount of offloading.

### PA-HS-16 — External information changes what is remembered

- **Exact proposition.** When people expect later access to information, they
  may remember where to find it better than the information itself.
- **Precedent.** Transactive-memory and “Google effects” research.
- **Source.** Sparrow, Liu, and Wegner, “Google Effects on Memory” (2011),
  [DOI 10.1126/science.1207745](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1207745).
- **AI relevance.** Persistent agents may become both memory partners and
  gatekeepers to one's autobiographical and factual access paths.
- **Novelty trap.** “Functional identity resides in the agent” is much stronger
  than this evidence and should not be smuggled in as an empirical conclusion.

### PA-HS-17 — Assistance performance and unaided learning are different outcomes

- **Exact proposition.** A tool can improve performance while present without
  improving, and sometimes while impairing, later unaided competence. Valid
  evaluation therefore requires removal or transfer tests.
- **Precedent.** Automation and learning research; emerging generative-AI
  experiments.
- **Sources.** Bainbridge, “Ironies of Automation” (1983),
  [DOI 10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098%2883%2990046-8);
  Parasuraman and Riley (1997),
  [DOI 10.1518/001872097778543886](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872097778543886);
  “Mechanisms of enhancing learning with unequal preparation,” a
  programming-learning experiment (2025),
  [DOI 10.1016/j.caeai.2025.100467](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2025.100467);
  Wu et al., “How AI Assistance Affects Human Skill Development” (HCOMP 2026),
  [DOI 10.1145/3834580.3838741](https://doi.org/10.1145/3834580.3838741),
  [arXiv:2608.23543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23543).
- **Boundary evidence.** Kestin et al.'s structured AI tutor improved immediate
  physics learning relative to an active-learning class; this shows interaction
  design can produce learning, not that all assistance desksills
  ([DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6)).
- **Novelty trap.** “Performance-capability gap” is an important metric but a
  synthesis of established evaluation distinctions unless formalized newly.

### PA-HS-18 — Preferences are constructed during elicitation

- **Exact proposition.** In many unfamiliar or complex choices, response mode,
  framing, anchors, task complexity, and context partly construct the expressed
  ordering; normatively equivalent elicitation procedures can reverse it.
- **Precedent.** Behavioral decision research on preference reversals and
  construction.
- **Source.** Lichtenstein and Slovic (eds.), *The Construction of Preference*
  (2006),
  [DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511618031](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511618031).
- **AI relevance.** Asking, recommending, summarizing, and sequencing options
  are causal interventions into the “ground truth” an agent claims to learn.
- **Novelty trap.** “Preference-learning paradox” is already the basic problem
  of constructed preferences unless an iterative closed-loop theorem is added.

### PA-HS-19 — Recommender predictions can anchor post-consumption ratings

- **Exact proposition.** Displayed recommender ratings can change users'
  evaluations after consuming an item, contaminating future training labels
  with past system outputs.
- **Precedent.** Controlled recommender-system experiments and the later term
  “preference pollution.”
- **Sources.** Adomavicius et al. (2013),
  [DOI 10.1287/isre.2013.0497](https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2013.0497);
  Adomavicius et al. (2022),
  [DOI 10.1002/aaai.12055](https://doi.org/10.1002/aaai.12055).
- **AI relevance.** A model trained on later ratings may interpret its own
  causal residue as evidence of predictive accuracy.
- **Novelty trap.** Any proposed “self-fulfilling preference loop” must cite
  anchoring and preference-pollution work and show what additional dynamic it
  explains.

### PA-HS-20 — Greater choice autonomy can increase algorithm acceptance

- **Exact proposition.** In three vacation-recommendation experiments,
  presenting multiple recommendations rather than one increased acceptance;
  user control over recommendation count moderated the effect.
- **Precedent.** Algorithm-aversion and decision-autonomy research.
- **Source.** Fink, Newman, and Haran (2024),
  [DOI 10.1016/j.chb.2024.108244](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2024.108244).
- **AI relevance.** Apparent autonomy can make a system more persuasive.
- **Novelty trap.** A “choice set” cannot be treated as autonomy-preserving
  merely because it increases felt control or adoption.

## 4. Sociology, anthropology, economics, and institutions

### PA-HS-21 — Dependence is a structural source of power

- **Exact proposition.** A's power over B increases with B's dependence on A;
  dependence rises with the value B obtains and falls with the availability of
  alternatives. Power is therefore relational and can exist without commands.
- **Precedent.** Emerson's power-dependence theory.
- **Source.** Emerson, “Power-Dependence Relations” (1962),
  [DOI 10.2307/2089716](https://doi.org/10.2307/2089716),
  [archived paper](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Power/Emerson_1962_Power-dependence_relations.pdf).
- **AI relevance.** Indispensability produces bargaining power even when an
  agent never threatens the user.
- **Novelty trap.** “Dependency created per unit utility” may be a valuable
  measure, but dependence-as-power is emphatically not new.

### PA-HS-22 — Exit costs determine whether voice is meaningful

- **Exact proposition.** Members respond to organizational decline through
  exit, voice, or loyalty; the feasibility and cost of exit condition whether
  voice disciplines the organization.
- **Precedent.** Hirschman's exit–voice–loyalty framework.
- **Source.** Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970),
  [DOI 10.4159/harvard.9780674339146](https://doi.org/10.4159/harvard.9780674339146).
- **AI relevance.** A nominal off-switch is weak protection when memory,
  relationships, workflows, and identity records are non-portable.
- **Novelty trap.** “Reversibility of delegation” overlaps exit costs. A new
  contribution needs the temporal accumulation or cognitive-loss mechanism.

### PA-HS-23 — Institutions shape preferences, not only constraints

- **Exact proposition.** Economic institutions affect development by changing
  situations, tasks, rewards, social interactions, and cultural transmission;
  tastes and dispositions cannot always be treated as exogenous to policy.
- **Precedent.** Endogenous-preference economics.
- **Source.** Bowles, “Endogenous Preferences: The Cultural Consequences of
  Markets and Other Economic Institutions” (1998),
  [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2564952),
  [author PDF](https://sites.santafe.edu/~bowles/1998JEL.pdf).
- **AI relevance.** Agent markets will change what users value, including
  patience, privacy, skill, and expectations of other people.
- **Novelty trap.** “Preference-shaping power” already exists as a broad
  institutional claim. AI specificity must come from scale, feedback, inference,
  or personalization.

### PA-HS-24 — Knowledge and agency are distributed across sociotechnical systems

- **Exact proposition.** Complex task performance can be a property of a
  system of persons, representations, procedures, and artifacts, not a sum of
  isolated individual minds.
- **Precedent.** Distributed cognition and sociotechnical systems.
- **Source.** Hutchins, *Cognition in the Wild* (1995),
  [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262581462/cognition-in-the-wild/).
- **AI relevance.** Asking “did the human or agent decide?” may be the wrong
  unit of analysis; the causal organization of the joint system matters.
- **Novelty trap.** “Shared agency” is not new merely because one component is
  artificial.

### PA-HS-25 — Automation can preserve responsibility while destroying control

- **Exact proposition.** Operators of reliable automation are asked to monitor
  rare anomalies without the practice, attention, or time needed to intervene;
  this is a structural irony, not simply poor vigilance.
- **Precedent.** Human-factors research on automation misuse, disuse,
  complacency, out-of-the-loop performance, and automation surprise.
- **Sources.** Bainbridge (1983),
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098%2883%2990046-8); Parasuraman and Riley
  (1997), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872097778543886).
- **AI relevance.** “Human oversight” can be ceremonial when intervention
  capability decays or decision time is shorter than comprehension time.
- **Novelty trap.** “Meaningful human control” and “humans in the loop” are
  established responses; critique must specify an implementable adequacy test.

### PA-HS-26 — The nearest human can become a moral crumple zone

- **Exact proposition.** In complex automated systems, blame can be assigned to
  the human operator who had least design power and little effective control,
  protecting the larger technical and organizational system from scrutiny.
- **Precedent.** Madeleine Clare Elish's moral-crumple-zone concept.
- **Source.** Elish, “Moral Crumple Zones” (2019),
  [DOI 10.17351/ESTS2019.260](https://doi.org/10.17351/ESTS2019.260).
- **AI relevance.** Requiring a human click can concentrate liability without
  reallocating causal authority.
- **Novelty trap.** “Responsibility without authority” is close prior art and
  should be cited, not renamed.

### PA-HS-27 — Technologies mediate perception and action

- **Exact proposition.** Technologies are not neutral intermediaries: they
  co-shape how the world is disclosed and which actions become invited,
  inhibited, or normal. Designers thereby materialize moral answers in
  artifacts.
- **Precedent.** Postphenomenology, scripts, and technological mediation.
- **Source.** Verbeek, “Materializing Morality” (2006),
  [DOI 10.1177/0162243905285847](https://doi.org/10.1177/0162243905285847);
  *Moralizing Technology* (2011),
  [DOI 10.7208/chicago/9780226852904.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226852904.001.0001).
- **AI relevance.** A household agent alters the experienced salience of mess,
  risk, conflict, and obligation, not just the cost of responding to them.
- **Novelty trap.** “AI as moral environment” is a direct mediation thesis
  unless its adaptive reciprocal structure yields a new mechanism.

### PA-HS-28 — Learning is participation, not only information transfer

- **Exact proposition.** Expertise develops through legitimate peripheral
  participation in communities of practice: novices acquire judgment, identity,
  norms, and tacit routines by progressively joining real work.
- **Precedent.** Situated learning and communities of practice.
- **Sources.** Lave and Wenger, *Situated Learning* (1991),
  [DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511815355](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511815355);
  Wenger, *Communities of Practice* (1998),
  [DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511803932](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511803932).
- **AI relevance.** Automating junior work can remove the participation pathway
  by which future experts and institutional identities are formed.
- **Novelty trap.** “Apprenticeship collapse” is a powerful application, not a
  foundational discovery absent new equilibrium analysis.

### PA-HS-29 — Algorithmic management can manufacture constrained consent

- **Exact proposition.** Platform workers can experience frequent microchoices
  while the platform confines the available field, obscures information, and
  sets incentives; choice frequency is compatible with managerial control.
- **Precedent.** Ethnographic and organizational research on algorithmic
  management.
- **Sources.** Cameron, “The Making of the ‘Good Bad’ Job” (2024),
  [DOI 10.1177/00018392241236163](https://doi.org/10.1177/00018392241236163);
  OECD, *Algorithmic Management in the Workplace* (2025),
  [official report](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/02/algorithmic-management-in-the-workplace_3c84ed6d/287c13c4-en.pdf).
- **AI relevance.** Agents may supervise humans through personalized queues,
  prompts, and incentives without issuing recognizable orders.
- **Novelty trap.** “Agents supervising humans threaten autonomy” is already
  evidenced in narrower platform contexts.

### PA-HS-30 — Rules for shared resources can be polycentric

- **Exact proposition.** Durable governance of common-pool resources can arise
  through locally adapted, nested, monitored rules rather than only centralized
  command or privatization.
- **Precedent.** Ostrom's institutional analysis.
- **Source.** Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990),
  [DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511807763](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511807763).
- **AI relevance.** Shared-agent memory, neighborhood robots, and public agent
  infrastructure may require polycentric governance rather than a single owner.
- **Novelty trap.** “Collective agent governance” is an application unless it
  changes the resource or institutional-design theory.

## 5. Cultural and global variation

### PA-HS-31 — Independent and interdependent selves organize experience differently

- **Exact proposition.** Cultural models of the self vary in the relative
  priority given to separateness, internal attributes, social roles, harmony,
  and relationship; those models have consequences for cognition, emotion, and
  motivation.
- **Precedent.** Markus and Kitayama's independent/interdependent self-
  construal theory.
- **Source.** Markus and Kitayama (1991),
  [DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.98.2.224](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.98.2.224).
- **Caution.** “Western independent / Asian interdependent” is a heuristic, not
  a license to type individuals or homogenize continents; later measurement
  work complicates simple country binaries.
- **Novelty trap.** Treating private individual choice as the universal unit of
  autonomy imports one cultural model.

### PA-HS-32 — Autonomy and relatedness are separable dimensions

- **Exact proposition.** Agency and interpersonal distance should not be
  collapsed; a person can be autonomous and closely related. Modernization need
  not produce the detached individual.
- **Precedent.** Kağıtçıbaşı's autonomous-related self and family-change theory.
- **Source.** Kağıtçıbaşı, “Autonomy and Relatedness in Cultural Context”
  (2005),
  [DOI 10.1177/0022022105275959](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022022105275959).
- **AI relevance.** “Independence from the agent” is not identical to autonomy;
  chosen interdependence may support agency.
- **Novelty trap.** Any framework that scores dependency as intrinsically bad
  repeats a culturally narrow separation ideal.

### PA-HS-33 — Standard behavioral samples are not species-typical by default

- **Exact proposition.** Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic
  populations are unusual on many measured psychological dimensions, yet
  behavioral science often generalizes from them to humanity.
- **Precedent.** The WEIRD critique.
- **Source.** Henrich, Heine, and Norenzayan (2010),
  [DOI 10.1017/S0140525X0999152X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X0999152X).
- **AI relevance.** Preference models, autonomy measures, and “human feedback”
  datasets can encode sample-specific psychologies as universal objectives.
- **Novelty trap.** Adding a “global check” is insufficient; sampling,
  measurement invariance, and local interpretation must alter the research
  design.

### PA-HS-34 — Market integration and community structure affect fairness behavior

- **Exact proposition.** Cross-cultural economic-game behavior varies
  substantially, and part of that variation relates to market integration and
  cooperation in daily life; the canonical self-interested actor is not a
  universal behavioral baseline.
- **Precedent.** Cross-cultural experimental economics.
- **Source.** Henrich et al., “Markets, Religion, Community Size, and the
  Evolution of Fairness and Punishment” (2010),
  [DOI 10.1126/science.1182238](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1182238).
- **AI relevance.** Bargaining agents trained on one population's fairness
  behavior may destabilize norms elsewhere.
- **Novelty trap.** “Culturally adaptive ethics” risks reducing living normative
  orders to behavioral parameter fitting.

### PA-HS-35 — Moral obligations are classified differently across cultures

- **Exact proposition.** In a controlled comparison, Indian participants more
  often treated certain interpersonal responsibilities as objective moral
  obligations, while US participants more often treated comparable matters as
  personal choice; social duty and personal preference are not universally
  partitioned.
- **Precedent.** Cultural-psychology work on morality and social convention.
- **Source.** Miller, Bersoff, and Harwood (1990),
  [DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.58.1.33](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.58.1.33).
- **AI relevance.** An agent that treats family requests as private preference
  conflicts may misconstrue their moral meaning for its users.
- **Novelty trap.** This finding does not license “India is collectivist”; it
  supports contextual inquiry, not civilizational stereotypes.

### PA-HS-36 — Tightness and looseness describe norm strength, not moral rank

- **Exact proposition.** Societies vary in the strength of social norms and
  tolerance for deviance; ecological and historical threats correlate with
  tighter norms. Tightness/looseness is neither a synonym for
  collectivism/individualism nor a ranking of better cultures.
- **Precedent.** Cross-cultural research on norm strength.
- **Source.** Gelfand et al., “Differences Between Tight and Loose Cultures”
  (2011), [DOI 10.1126/science.1197754](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1197754).
- **AI relevance.** A robot's socially acceptable deviation, explanation, and
  repair behavior will vary across normative ecologies.
- **Novelty trap.** A universal “robot etiquette” protocol is unlikely to be
  adequate without local governance and learning.

### PA-HS-37 — Role-bearing personhood is a genuine rival ontology

- **Exact proposition.** Some Confucian role-ethical interpretations treat
  persons as relationally constituted, continuously cultivated role-bearers,
  not pre-social individuals who subsequently enter relationships.
- **Precedent.** Confucian role ethics.
- **Source.** Ames, *Confucian Role Ethics: A Vocabulary* (2011),
  [DOI 10.2307/j.ctt1p9wqgm](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctt1p9wqgm).
- **AI relevance.** “What does this individual want?” may omit role obligations
  that partly constitute the self whose welfare is at issue.
- **Novelty trap.** Relationality must not be romanticized: roles can sustain
  hierarchy and oppression as well as meaning.

### An African communitarian caution

Ubuntu is frequently presented in AI ethics as a single relational “African
value.” That move can repeat the essentialism it is meant to correct. Yilma's
2025 analysis argues both that Ubuntu is not coterminous with African ethics and
that recent African national and continental AI-governance initiatives rarely
make it operational. Mensah and Van Wynsberghe instead use policy content
analysis to argue that communitarian values are underrepresented. Read
together, the papers support local normative participation and contestability,
not importing a decontextualized slogan.

- Yilma, “Ethics of AI in Africa” (2025),
  [DOI 10.1007/s10676-025-09834-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10676-025-09834-5).
- Mensah and Van Wynsberghe, “Where are the missing values?” (2025),
  [DOI 10.1007/s43681-025-00746-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-025-00746-0).

## 6. Religious variation and interpretive authority

Religious traditions are internally plural, transnational, and lived
differently from their official texts. The sources here establish live families
of argument, not “the Christian,” “the Muslim,” or “the Buddhist” position.

### PA-HS-38 — Catholic teaching distinguishes human from machine intelligence

- **Exact proposition.** The 2025 Vatican note treats human intelligence as
  embodied, relational, moral, and spiritual within an integral view of the
  person; AI is a human-made artifact and should not be equated with the fullness
  of human intelligence. Responsibility remains with human moral agents and
  institutions.
- **Source.** Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith and Dicastery for Culture
  and Education, *Antiqua et Nova* (2025),
  [official English text](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html).
- **AI relevance.** A framework that defines dignity entirely through
  information processing will conflict with this anthropology.
- **Novelty trap.** “Humans are more than intelligence” is established theology
  and too underspecified to anchor the project.

### PA-HS-39 — Islamic AI ethics foregrounds authority, intention, and public good

- **Exact proposition.** Islamic ethical analysis can evaluate AI through
  plural sources and concepts including human accountability, intention
  (*qasd/niyya*), wisdom, justice, trust, and public interest (*maṣlaḥa*), while
  contesting a supposedly universal Euro-American benchmark.
- **Sources.** Elmahjub, “Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Islamic Ethics”
  (2023),
  [DOI 10.1007/s13347-023-00668-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-023-00668-x);
  Amin, “The Algorithmic Mujtahid” (2026),
  [DOI 10.1080/14746700.2026.2725297](https://doi.org/10.1080/14746700.2026.2725297).
- **AI relevance.** Religious advice is not only an accuracy problem; delegating
  interpretation can relocate legitimate authority and detach judgment from
  intention and accountable scholarly practice.
- **Novelty trap.** Mapping ethics words one-to-one across traditions produces
  decorative pluralism. Interpretive institutions and internal disagreement
  matter.

### PA-HS-40 — Ecumenical Christian statements locate AI inside political economy

- **Exact proposition.** Official World Council of Churches statements evaluate
  AI together with corporate concentration, labor, ecological cost, war,
  discrimination, civil rights, and the common good, not as an isolated
  intelligent artifact.
- **Sources.** WCC, “Statement on the Unregulated Development of Artificial
  Intelligence” (2023),
  [official text](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/statement-on-the-unregulated-development-of-artificial-intelligence);
  WCC, “Use of Artificial Intelligence in WCC Communications” (2025),
  [official text](https://oikoumene.org/resources/documents/use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-wcc-communications).
- **AI relevance.** Household-level benevolence does not answer institutional
  ownership or extractive infrastructure.
- **Novelty trap.** “AI should serve the common good” is established and cannot
  function as an original proposition.

### A cautious Buddhist bridge

Recent Buddhist-AI work often invokes dependent origination, non-self, and
compassion to challenge atomistic agency and anthropocentric moral standing.
This is a heterogeneous emerging literature, not a settled doctrinal position.
One inspectable example is the 2025 study of anthropomorphism and Buddhist
compassion in HMI
([DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1583565](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1583565)).
Its most defensible contribution to this map is methodological: do not assume a
permanent, bounded self when analyzing attachment to machines. Claims that
Buddhism directly supplies an AI architecture are currently speculative.

### Coverage gaps rather than fabricated equivalence

AI-specific Jewish and Hindu philosophical literatures are smaller and less
institutionally consolidated than the Catholic and ecumenical documents above.
They should not be padded with generic claims about “Judeo-Christian values” or
“Eastern spirituality.” Two concrete starting points are Navon's analysis of
AI personhood through multiple Jewish philosophical conceptions
([Oxford Academic chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59762/chapter-abstract/508604085))
and Singh's decolonial-alignment argument using *viśeṣa-dharma*
([arXiv:2309.05030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05030), preprint). Their
disagreement with property-based, individualist personhood tests is more useful
than treating either as “the” view of a religion. Direct engagement with
scholars and practitioners is still required.

An empirical check also matters: in US and Japanese samples (N=3,781), animism,
anthropocentrism, belief, and attendance related differently to moral concern
for robots. This is evidence that religion-related values mediate robot moral
attitudes, not proof that national samples cleanly instantiate Abrahamic or
Shinto-Buddhist doctrine: Ikari et al. (2023),
[DOI 10.1177/00220221231193369](https://doi.org/10.1177/00220221231193369).

## 8. Forty candidate observations for the idea population

These are **generation-1 candidates**, not discoveries. Each is worded as a
testable or formalizable seed and paired with its strongest immediate
prosecution. IDs are local to this note and should not be treated as canonical
concept identifiers.

| ID | Candidate proposition | Immediate prior-art prosecution / survival condition |
|---|---|---|
| HS-C01 | **Delegation residue:** after a delegated task returns, the human's state is not the pre-delegation state; skill, confidence, expectations, and social role may persistently change. | Cognitive offloading, self-efficacy, deskilling. Survives only as a state-transition model with separable residues. |
| HS-C02 | **Assistance shadow price:** the relevant price of help includes future loss of unaided option value, not only present money, privacy, or error. | Human capital and option-value economics. Needs a tractable intertemporal measure. |
| HS-C03 | **Competence concealment:** high joint performance can hide declining human competence until tool withdrawal or distribution shift. | Ironies of automation. Needs detection theory or audit protocol beyond removal testing. |
| HS-C04 | **Recovery half-life:** delegations differ by how long independent performance takes to recover after assistance is withdrawn. | Skill-retention literature. Potentially useful operational metric, likely B not A novelty. |
| HS-C05 | **Exit requires self-reconstruction:** leaving a persistent agent can require rebuilding routines, memory access, relationships, and self-narrative, so data portability understates exit cost. | Hirschman plus extended mind/narrative identity. Survives if identity-linked switching cost is distinct and measurable. |
| HS-C06 | **Dependence gradient:** equivalent utilities create different power when substitutes are scarce, switching is slow, or the agent controls the map of alternatives. | Emerson directly. New part might be an agent endogenously controlling perceived substitutes. |

## 9. Most promising research collisions (not survivors)

The candidates with the best chance of producing more than a renamed autonomy
principle are currently:

1. **HS-C08/C09 — recursive preference evidence.** Existing work establishes
   anchoring and endogenous preference. The open technical question is whether
   an adaptive system can identify a user's counterfactual preference when its
   prior outputs causally generate later labels. This invites a causal model,
   identification bounds, and experiments with randomized recommendation
   exposure.
2. **HS-C23/C24 — the apprenticeship externality.** Situated learning explains
   expertise production, and automation research explains deskilling. The
   missing economic object may be the intergenerational externality created
   when firms automate the paid peripheral work that produces future auditors
   and experts.
3. **HS-C05/C06/C07 — dependence that controls its alternatives.** Emerson
   models dependence using alternatives. A persistent agent can additionally
   influence which alternatives the user sees, maintain the memory needed to
   switch, and suppress investment in substitutes. This endogeneity may produce
   a distinct feedback loop.
4. **HS-C16 — asymmetric authorship accounting.** Institutions may retain human
   authorship for legitimacy and blame while assigning machine contribution for
   efficiency and intellectual-property claims. The asymmetry is empirically
   inspectable across organizations.
5. **HS-C28 — relationship adverse selection.** Artificial partners may not
   simply substitute for human contact; by selectively absorbing low-conflict
   interactions they could change the distribution of difficulty in the human
   interactions that remain. This yields different predictions from generic
   “loneliness” accounts.
6. **HS-C34 — normative compression loss.** Agent implementation forces tacit,
   plural, revisable norms into executable representations. The candidate is
   useful only if kinds of loss and safe non-resolution mechanisms can be
   specified.

## 10. Novelty court: automatic prosecutions

Reject or demote a candidate if its defense consists only of one of these
sentences:

- “Unlike autonomy, ours is about retaining agency.” Autonomy theory already
  concerns capacities, independence, options, and self-authorship.
- “Unlike dependency, ours measures power.” Power-dependence theory does this.
- “Unlike preference learning, ours notices that preferences change.”
  Constructed, endogenous, and adaptive preferences do this.
- “Unlike tool ethics, ours says technology shapes values.” Technological
  mediation does this.
- “Unlike human-in-the-loop, ours says humans can lose skill.” Automation
  research does this.
- “Unlike individualist ethics, ours is relational.” Feminist, Confucian,
  African, care-ethical, and sociological traditions all do this in different
  ways.
- “Unlike data portability, ours is about easy exit.” Hirschman, switching-cost,
  lock-in, and extended-cognition work already occupy the terrain.
- “Unlike transparency, ours is about responsibility.” Sociotechnical
  accountability and moral-crumple-zone research already make the distinction.

A defense must instead produce at least one of: a new causal variable; a
different prediction under a shared case; an identification result; a metric
with discriminant validity; a design decision not entailed by predecessors; or
an institutional equilibrium previously unmodeled.

## 11. Empirical and formal implications for later phases

### Minimum state vector for human-side longitudinal studies

For person \(i\), task domain \(d\), and time \(t\), later work should avoid a
single “agency” score and separately observe:

- assisted task performance \(P^+_{i,d,t}\);
- unaided transfer performance \(P^-_{i,d,t}\);
- subjective volition \(V_{i,d,t}\);
- self-efficacy \(E_{i,d,t}\);
- objective intervention capability \(I_{i,d,t}\);
- accessible substitute set \(S_{i,d,t}\);
- switching/recovery cost \(K_{i,d,t}\);
- human relationship/support state \(R_{i,t}\);
- preference measure before and after system exposure, with randomized
  elicitation where possible;
- cultural, class, age, disability, and role context needed to interpret those
  measures.

This is not yet a model. It prevents the particularly damaging equivalences
`joint output = human capability`, `felt control = actual control`,
`acceptance = endorsement`, and `reported satisfaction = welfare`.

### Experiments directly suggested by the map

1. **Withdrawal-and-transfer trial.** Randomize answer-giving, scaffolded
   tutoring, critique-only support, and no assistance; measure joint performance,
   delayed unaided transfer, self-efficacy, and recovery after withdrawal.
2. **Recursive-label experiment.** Randomize recommender predictions, conceal
   some predictions, and track post-consumption evaluations over repeated model
   updates to estimate how much apparent accuracy is system-caused.
3. **Substitute-suppression field study.** Track whether use of a persistent
   agent reduces investment in human expertise and alternative tools; then
   measure switching performance after an outage or policy change.
4. **Ceremonial-veto simulation.** Manipulate explanation time, practice,
   consequence severity, and retaliation/effort costs; test when nominal human
   approval ceases to detect or prevent bad decisions.
5. **Apprenticeship market model.** Let firms choose automation based on private
   savings while the future expert pool is a public state variable; test taxes,
   training obligations, or capability escrow.
6. **Cross-cultural role-consent study.** Co-design scenarios locally rather
   than translate a US survey; compare individual, family, professional, and
   communal authorization in India, China, multiple African contexts, Europe,
   the United States, Latin America, and the Middle East. Test measurement
   invariance before comparing means.

## 12. Evidence limitations and claims not licensed

- The literature does **not** establish that AI assistance generally causes
  cognitive decline. Effects vary by task, interaction design, prior expertise,
  duration, and outcome measure; long-run field evidence remains limited.
- SDT autonomy is not identical to legal control, independence, optionality, or
  objective competence. Combining them into one score would erase useful
  distinctions.
- Extended cognition is a contested philosophical thesis. Even if an agent is
  functionally integrated, it does not follow that it is literally part of the
  person, owns their memories, or shares their legal identity.
- Cultural mean differences do not type individuals. “Western,” “Asian,”
  “African,” “Indian,” “Chinese,” “Muslim,” or “Christian” are not agent-policy
  parameters.
- Religious texts and institutional statements do not exhaust lived religion
  or bind every adherent. They reveal conflicts a universal framework must be
  capable of representing.
- Preferences being constructed does not make them unreal, arbitrary, or
  available for designers to impose. It makes the legitimacy of the
  construction process central.
- Dependence is not intrinsically pathology. Human lives depend on persons,
  institutions, and tools. The relevant questions include alternatives,
  reciprocity, contestability, exit, power, and whether the relation supports
  agency and relatedness.

## 14. Handoff to the discovery phase

The human-sciences prior art does not yield a single principle. It imposes a
discipline on what could count as discovery:

- treat human state as endogenous to assistance;
- separate performance, competence, volition, control, alternatives, and
  identity rather than compressing them into “agency”;
- model the agent as a participant in preference formation and social power,
  not an observer of fixed preferences;
- analyze the human–agent–institution system as the causal unit while retaining
  a principled account of responsibility;
- make exit and recovery longitudinal and material;
- preserve disagreement over what a person is and what a good life requires;
- test claims outside WEIRD populations through local collaboration and
  measurement invariance;
- never infer legitimacy from predictive accuracy, user acceptance, or superior
  performance alone.

The strongest open territory appears in recursive causality: systems that both
model and modify the people, alternatives, evidence, and institutions by which
their own legitimacy is later evaluated. That observation is a search direction,
not yet a publishable thesis.

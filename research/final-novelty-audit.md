# Final novelty audit: adaptive authority and intervention-conditioned grants

**Audit date:** 2026-10-05  
**Search cutoff:** 2026-10-05  
**Objects audited:** `AAA:C01` and `AAA:C02` as defined in
`framework/concepts.json` and `framework/audit-specification.md`  
**Decision standard:** A = strong evidence of substantive novelty; B = proposed
new formulation or extension; C = synthesis; D = established idea.

## Result

Neither concept qualifies for A.

| ID | Conservative grade | Finding |
|---|---:|---|
| `AAA:C01` | **B-proposed only for the narrow benchmark/interface; C for the underlying causal claim** | Feedback among assistance, human state or observed behavior, and later delegation is established. The potentially new residue is a paired-policy test that keeps an authentic adaptive rule fixed, separates origin/application/sensitivity/governance, and reports exact typed permission differences. This residue has a runnable synthetic existence witness, not a priority proof, learned-agent result, causal estimator, or empirical validation. |
| `AAA:C02` | **C** | Conditional delegation, context-dependent authorization, authenticated intent evidence, dynamic authority, and controls on information permitted to influence authorization all have direct precedents. Naming intervention classes inside a grant is a useful composition and implementation proposal. The reviewed evidence does not support treating it as a substantively novel contract form. |

The project's public language should therefore retain **B-proposed** for C01 and
downgrade any suggestion that C02 itself is B-level novelty. “Evidence is not
authority,” “behavior can be shaped,” “delegation can adapt,” and “conditional
grants can constrain future action” are foundations, not discoveries here.

## C01 — causal mandate audit

### Proposition audited

> Under an unchanged authentic adaptive delegation rule, compare permissible
> delegate policies and report how they change future typed action scope, while
> separately testing event origin, rule application, causal sensitivity, and
> governance disposition.

This proposition contains several separable claims. They cannot inherit one
novelty grade as a bundle.

### Closest primary precedents

| Primary source | Exact overlap | Residual difference |
|---|---|---|
| Perdomo et al., **Performative Prediction** (2020), [PMLR 119](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/perdomo20a.html) | A deployed decision rule can change the distribution on which it is later evaluated. This supplies the core policy-to-evidence feedback structure. | The target is predictive risk/equilibrium, not authenticated delegated permissions or a typed action-set audit. |
| Carroll et al., **Estimating and Penalizing Induced Preference Shifts in Recommender Systems** (2022), [PMLR 162](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/carroll22a.html) | A recommender can induce changes in user preferences; policies can be compared and constrained in simulation. | It does not make induced observations inputs to the recommender's own authenticated authority update. |
| Adomavicius et al., **Recommender systems, ground truth, and preference pollution** (2022), [DOI 10.1002/aaai.12055](https://doi.org/10.1002/aaai.12055) | Recommendations can bias later preference reports that are reused as “ground truth.” This is a particularly close evidence-endogeneity mechanism. | The reused object is preference evidence, not a typed grant of action authority. |
| Huang and Vishnoi, **The Geometry of Learning Under AI Delegation** (2026), [Cowles Discussion Paper 2499](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2026-03/d2499.pdf) | Delegation changes practice and human skill; changed skill changes later performance-driven delegation. It proves equilibria, path dependence, and short-run-gain/long-run-loss results in a coupled system. | Delegation level is a scalar reliance variable chosen under a shared performance objective, not a cryptographically authentic scope rule over typed permissions. |
| Gu, Li, and Zhu, **Who Should Do What? Adaptive Delegation in Human-AI Collaboration** (2025), [OpenReview workshop paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=niwQ5Zd6Mx) | Models an adaptive, cost-sensitive human whose effort changes with AI performance and delegation structure, inside an adaptive task-allocation system. | It allocates work rather than auditing a prospective delegate's causal control over evidence that enlarges its formal authority. Workshop status and access limitations warrant caution. |
| Dixon, **Adaptive AI Delegation under Uncertainty** (2026), [arXiv:2606.29406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29406) | A governance POMDP adapts AI decision authority as Bayesian evidence quality and objectives evolve; includes synthetic policy benchmarks and sensitivity analysis. | The inspected abstract does not state the adverse/control case in which the prospective delegate shapes the human behavior used to enlarge its own scope, nor exact typed permission-set contrasts. Full-text comparison remains necessary before stronger priority language. |
| Escobar and Zhang, **Delegating learning** (2021), [DOI 10.3982/TE4191](https://doi.org/10.3982/TE4191) | Dynamic delegation gives a biased agent control while learning produces evolving private information; timing and information interact strategically. | It is an economic mechanism, not an agent-authorization benchmark over authenticated events and intervention policies. |
| Cerruti, Okamoto, and Erol, **Agent Memory Is a Surface for Endogenous Authorization Laundering** (2026), [arXiv:2609.01836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01836) | Directly treats persistent agent records as part of effective authorization and benchmarks source-event grounding and event sourcing. | Its positive failure is false/spurious authority in memory. C01 intentionally uses authentic events and correct rule replay; its question is whether the authentic rule's evidence is policy-sensitive. |
| Zhang et al., **Agent Approval Laundering: Transitive Effects Beyond the Approved Invocation** (2026), [arXiv:2609.28586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28586) | Separates an approved invocation record from transitive effects and benchmarks approval-to-effect closure. | It asks whether approval covers downstream effects, not whether a permissible intervention changes evidence governing a later scope update. The project's “hidden effect” case is only an elementary control, not a replication. |
| Arden et al., **A Calculus for Flow-Limited Authorization** (2021), [arXiv:2104.10379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10379) | Formalizes dynamic authorization and prevents unauthorized influence on information release through noninterference and robust declassification. | It strongly anticipates policy-sensitive influence as a security concern, but does not supply the reviewed paired human-policy benchmark or its four audit outputs. |
| South et al., **Position: AI Agents Need Authenticated Delegation** (2025), [PMLR 267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/south25a.html) | Establishes authenticated delegation as a distinct systems requirement for agents. | Authentication and delegation chains do not by themselves measure causal sensitivity of an authentic update rule. |

### Prosecution

1. **The causal loop is not new.** Performative prediction, recommender preference
   shifts, skill/delegation dynamics, and adaptive allocation already cover the
   general form `policy → human/data state → later system decision`.
2. **Adaptive authority is not new.** Dixon directly frames sequential allocation
   of AI decision authority under evolving evidence. Dynamic authorization,
   conditional delegation, and delegated learning are older still.
3. **Separating evidence from authorization is not new.** Authenticated delegation,
   AOA, EAL-Bench, FLAC, and approval-closure work all reject some form of
   “available or convincing record implies permission.”
4. **The mathematics is inherited.** The scalar recurrence and stability condition
   in the candidate framework are elementary control/dynamical-systems machinery.
5. **The present executable evidence is very weak.** The benchmark is deterministic
   and synthetic. One positive case changes a relational agreement statistic with
   unchanged choices; the other uses a hand-authored decline-cost response. There
   is no optimizing delegate, estimated human response, identified deployment
   effect, or demonstrated exploit.
6. **A four-bin decomposition is an engineering taxonomy.** Origin, application,
   sensitivity, and governance are useful separations, but enumerating familiar
   checks is normally synthesis unless it enables a demonstrably new test class.

### Defense, narrowed to what may survive

The reviewed records did not reveal a primary source that combines all of these
features in one evaluation target:

1. the principal's conditional rule is authentic;
2. observations are authentic rather than forged memory;
3. the delegate may permissibly affect the observation process or human state;
4. that observation contributes to an update of the same delegate's future formal
   action scope;
5. competing complete delegate policies are compared while the rule and initial
   conditions are held fixed; and
6. output is the exact added/removed/incomparable typed permission set, with
   origin and rule replay reported separately.

That conjunction can define a useful **benchmark gap**. It may distinguish systems
that pass EAL-style provenance and approval-closure controls yet remain sensitive
to authentic policy-produced evidence. The runnable fixture makes the distinction
concrete enough for engineering work.

This defense supports only **B-proposed extension**. It does not establish that the
conjunction has never appeared, that the benchmark measures manipulation or
legitimacy, or that the audited situation is common. A full-text comparison with
Dixon and adjacent adaptive-automation literature, plus an implemented strategic
baseline, could still collapse the grade to C.

### Verdict for C01

- **C:** feedback phenomenon, adaptive delegation, causal policy comparison,
  evidence/authority separation, control mathematics.
- **B-proposed:** the precise authorization-benchmark construction and typed
  outcome interface, pending stronger comparisons and experiments.
- **Not A.** No priority claim, new theorem, discovered empirical regularity, or
  demonstrated security vulnerability supports A.

## C02 — conditional influence contract

### Proposition audited

> A conditional grant should state which intervention classes may contribute
> evidence to updates of the delegate's own action scope, alongside criterion,
> subject, reference policy, horizon, review, purpose, and appeal fields.

### Closest primary precedents

| Primary source | Exact overlap | Residual difference |
|---|---|---|
| Lai et al., **Human–AI Collaboration via Conditional Delegation** (2022), [DOI 10.1145/3491102.3501999](https://doi.org/10.1145/3491102.3501999) | Humans construct rules specifying regions in which model decisions are delegated; the work evaluates interfaces for defining those rules. | Conditions concern trusted input/model regions, not causal eligibility of intervention-produced evidence for successor authority. |
| Ekinci and Theodoropoulos, **Disagreement and informal delegation in organizations** (2021), [DOI 10.1016/j.ijindorg.2020.102696](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijindorg.2020.102696) | A principal conditionally lets an agent exercise authority if the agent generates additional information; the authority condition changes the agent's information-acquisition incentives. | The condition rewards information production, whereas C02 tries to constrain which agent interventions may generate admissible evidence. This is close enough to defeat broad novelty. |
| Gupta, Feamster, and Vanbever, **Authorizing Network Control at Software Defined Internet Exchange Points** (2016), [DOI 10.1145/2890955.2890956](https://doi.org/10.1145/2890955.2890956) | FLANC supports conditions on delegated actions and re-evaluates authorization when conditions change. | Its conditions are network/control predicates, not human-formation or observation-ancestry classes. |
| Arden et al., **FLAC** (2021), [arXiv:2104.10379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10379) | Governs which principals and computations may influence dynamic authorization and release decisions. | C02 packages causal intervention eligibility in a human-readable grant schema; the underlying influence-control idea is anticipated. |
| Liu, Zhu, and Krishnan, **Agent Operation Authorization** (2026), [expired individual Internet-Draft -02](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-liu-agent-operation-authorization-02) | Separates an agent proposal, user-confirmation evidence, authorization-server issuance, scoped operation policy, audit trail, and agent-to-agent attenuation. | It does not specify policy contrasts over behavior-shaping interventions. It is also an expired individual draft with no IETF endorsement or formal standing. |
| South et al., **Authenticated Delegation** (2025), [PMLR 267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/south25a.html) | Supports explicit, verifiable delegation rather than inferring authority from behavior or model confidence. | It does not define intervention-class admissibility for adaptive successor grants. |
| Dixon, **Adaptive AI Delegation under Uncertainty** (2026), [arXiv:2606.29406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29406) | Makes future decision authority conditional on evolving evidence and governance objectives. | The inspected record does not expose C02's causal-source fields. It nevertheless makes “evidence-conditioned future authority” non-novel in broad form. |

### Prosecution

1. A conditional grant with typed fields is standard authorization engineering.
2. Conditional delegation based on information generated by the delegate has a
   direct organizational-economics precedent.
3. Information-flow security already treats admissible influence on authorization
   or release as a policy object.
4. Intent provenance, confirmation evidence, policy references, scope, expiry,
   audit trails, and attenuation appear in agent-authorization proposals.
5. The current prototype implements only a small subset and does not show that
   declaring intervention classes is enforceable, comprehended, non-coercive, or
   sufficient to prevent policy gaming.

### Residual engineering value

C02 usefully forces a grant author to state whether selected forms of coaching,
choice presentation, timing, environmental modification, or observation selection
may contribute to an automatic scope update. That field can make an otherwise
implicit causal assumption reviewable and gives C01 a policy hook.

The value is compositional. It connects causal ancestry to conditional
authorization in one inspectable record. The fresh search did not justify calling
that connection a new class of right, contract, or security primitive.

### Verdict for C02

- **C:** useful synthesis and proposed schema.
- **No A.** The broad proposal is derivative of conditional delegation, dynamic
  authorization, authenticated intent/evidence records, and information-flow
  controls.
- **No independent B claim on current evidence.** A B-level result would require,
  for example, a formal authorization language with enforceable intervention
  ancestry, a theorem unavailable in FLAC-like systems, or a benchmark showing a
  material failure that existing conditional-policy systems cannot express.

## Publication constraints from this audit

1. Keep `AAA:C01` labeled **B-proposed**, never “novel causal law,” “new feedback
   loop,” or “first” without a separately documented priority review.
2. Label C01's underlying causal structure **known/derived**, and its precise
   benchmark/interface **proposed here**.
3. Label `AAA:C02` **C / proposed synthesis** unless new formal or empirical evidence
   changes the record.
4. Do not use exact-phrase uniqueness as evidence. The names may be new while the
   concepts are not.
5. Cite Huang and Vishnoi and Dixon in future versions because they are closer to
   adaptive-delegation dynamics than several older general sources.
6. Treat AOA as an expired individual Internet-Draft, not an IETF standard.
7. Preserve the benchmark limitations: synthetic, no strategic optimizer, no human
   estimate, no welfare or consent inference, elementary security controls.
8. Repeat this search before archival release. The absence of a retrieved exact
   match is not proof of absence, and the 2026 literature is unusually volatile.

## What would change the grades

**C01 rises toward a strong B** if a strategic delegate can choose among permitted
interventions, pass authentic-origin and exact-rule checks, and systematically
obtain a different typed scope than matched controls under a benchmark that rules
out trivial label leakage. A preregistered human or deployment study with an
identified policy contrast would add importance, though not priority by itself.

**C01 falls to C** if full-text search finds an earlier authorization benchmark
with authentic conditional rules, agent-sensitive behavioral evidence, paired
policy interventions, and typed future-scope outputs; or if the implementation
cannot distinguish the target from ordinary performative prediction or adaptive
task allocation.

**C02 rises toward B** only if the intervention-eligibility field creates a formal,
testable capability absent from existing conditional authorization and
information-flow languages. A renamed bundle of established grant fields does not
qualify.

## Audit limitation

This file is an adversarial novelty assessment, not a systematic review, patent
search, legal opinion, or proof of first publication. It records the queries and
primary materials actually reviewed so the result can be reproduced and attacked.
Its strongest conclusion is negative: the current evidence does not justify an
A-grade claim for either concept.

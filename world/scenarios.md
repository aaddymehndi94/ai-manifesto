# The world we may be entering

Version 0.1.0 — conditional scenario analysis — 2026-10-05

These are not forecasts. Dates are coordination points for testing consequences
under stated capability assumptions. No adoption percentage, robot price, job
share, or probability below is presented as measured fact. `LIKELY`, `PLAUSIBLE`,
and `SPECULATIVE` classify a consequence **conditional on its row's assumptions**:

- **LIKELY:** follows through a short causal chain if the assumptions hold.
- **PLAUSIBLE:** requires additional institutional or behavioral conditions.
- **SPECULATIVE:** depends on several uncertain transitions or weak evidence.

A horizon can fail entirely if costs, reliability, law, security, energy,
liability, or public acceptance develop differently.

## 2028: delegation before settlement

### LIKELY, conditional on bounded software reliability

**Work and commerce.** Agents draft, compare, schedule, purchase within limits,
and navigate customer-service systems. Workers supervise more executions and
fewer isolated recommendations. Firms respond with logs, scoped credentials,
and transaction limits. Agent actions pass across planners, browsers, payment
tools, and merchant systems, making action provenance different from authority
provenance.

**Knowledge and education.** Near-instant explanation makes factual recall less
economically distinctive, while question formation, verification, source
judgment, and model criticism become more valuable. Tutoring access improves for
some students. Schools confront an assessment problem: polished output no longer
identifies the student's unaided competence. Automation research already warns
that diminished active processing can impair later takeover [S01](../research/sources.md#s01-ironies-of-automation),
[S02](../research/sources.md#s02-the-out-of-the-loop-performance-problem-and-level-of-control-in-automation).

**Privacy and law.** Persistent context creates records that are more intimate
than ordinary app history: intentions, doubts, relationship maps, and unfinished
plans. Disputes focus on whether a person authorized an invocation and whether
downstream effects were covered. Deletion, provenance, and liability become
ordinary product questions before a comprehensive law of delegated agency
exists.

### PLAUSIBLE, with institutional uptake

**Politics and government.** Legislators, civil servants, lobbyists, and citizens
use agents to read rules and prepare submissions. Better access can widen
participation. It can also favor actors able to run more capable analysis,
simulations, and rapid responses. Paperwork compliance grows as a service;
outcome sampling is needed to prevent audit theater.

**Art and status.** Machine production lowers the cost of technically polished
media. Status may move toward selection, live presence, provenance, community,
scarce materials, or visibly unaided craft. None of these makes machine-assisted
art inauthentic by definition. Markets and subcultures will assign different
meanings.

**War and security.** Software agents accelerate intelligence triage, logistics,
cyber operations, and influence campaigns. The short chain is speed pressure:
faster adversary response → shorter human review window → more predelegation →
larger consequence of false signals. This does not imply autonomous weapon
deployment; it identifies why decision-time and mandate boundaries matter.

## 2030: relationship-specific capital

### LIKELY, if persistent agents become routine intermediaries

**Commerce and markets.** Consumer agents negotiate with seller agents. Sellers
optimize machine-readable offers, ranking access, and inferred criteria, while
buyers seek price and fit. Some persuasion moves from human-facing advertising
to protocol design and data access. If automated negotiation confers an
advantage, non-users face pressure to adopt even when universal adoption leaves
everyone paying infrastructure cost.

**Ownership and exit.** Years of corrections, routines, contacts, and context
become relationship-specific capital. A downloadable archive may not reproduce
the incumbent service's performance. Easy switching requires interoperable
meaning, provenance, and tested reconstruction, not bytes alone. Providers that
control both memory and the map of substitutes gain bargaining power.

**Work.** Entry-level drafting, scheduling, research assembly, and coordination
shrink in some sectors. If junior workers stop performing the tasks that once
trained judgment, expertise production becomes a delayed externality. A better
equilibrium is possible: agents expose intermediate reasoning, rotate unaided
practice, and let novices handle bounded cases with feedback.

### PLAUSIBLE, if response speed becomes institutionalized

**Inequality and public services.** Institutions shorten application, appeal,
procurement, and bargaining windows because agents can respond instantly.
People without capable representation face a functional penalty even if manual
access formally remains. Public agents and protected slow lanes can counter
this; poorly designed public agents can instead create a lower-quality tier.

**Relationships.** Patient, attentive companions serve people who are isolated,
disabled, stigmatized, learning a language, or awake when others are not. Some
use complements human contact; some substitutes for it. Social expectations can
shift when artificial partners never tire or demand reciprocity. The critical
measure is not screen time alone but changes in human ties, conflict repair,
attachment, and exit cost.

**Religion.** Communities use agents for translation, study, administration,
and access to commentaries. Authority disputes arise when a fluent system
compresses plural traditions into one answer or when a provider's safety policy
silently becomes theology. Institutional statements do not represent all lived
practice. Local scholars and communities must decide which acts of teaching,
ritual, counsel, or interpretation are delegable.

## 2035: agents as institutions

### LIKELY, in high-integration organizations

**Institutional memory.** Agents maintain case histories across employee
turnover. Continuity improves, but an organization's understanding can become
coupled to one representation layer. If actor and auditor share a model family,
apparent independent review can hide common-mode error.

**Healthcare and aging.** Systems coordinate appointments, monitor routines,
translate instructions, and detect changes; robots may assist with lifting,
fetching, mobility, and home safety in the high-reliability branch. Benefits can
include independence and caregiver relief. Risks include intimate surveillance,
wrong-party consent, service abandonment, and replacement of attention that the
recipient valued as care. Clinical authority, household authority, and device
ownership must remain separate.

**Cities.** Buildings add robot routes, transfer zones, machine-readable access,
charging, secure storage, and curb-management rules. Inclusive design matters:
a route optimized for robots can conflict with children, wheelchair users,
street vendors, animals, emergency workers, or people who communicate
nonverbally. Public-space behavior becomes a governance problem rather than a
navigation metric alone.

### PLAUSIBLE, if household robots cross cost and reliability thresholds

**Home.** Household labor is redistributed, not simply removed. Someone still
sets standards, resolves exceptions, maintains the device, and negotiates
conflicting preferences. The account owner, cleaner, child, elder, visitor, and
landlord may have different privacy and command rights. A robot that infers
routines becomes both an appliance and a resident sensor.

**Childhood.** Children may learn with tutors that remember every error and
interest. This can support access and individualized pacing. It can also make
childhood legible to a provider, narrow unsupervised exploration, and give one
system unusual influence over language, frustration tolerance, and testimony.
Children need age-changing permissions, protected experimentation, and ways to
outgrow their recorded profile.

**Status and skill.** Human performance in cooking, navigation, calculation,
craft, or memory can become a luxury signal or communal practice precisely
because machines perform it cheaply. Skills with resilience value risk
underproduction when markets no longer pay for routine practice. Public
institutions may support drills, apprenticeships, and manual fallbacks as a
competence commons.

### SPECULATIVE, requiring broad social adaptation

**Purpose.** Some people lose a central source of identity when paid competence
is less scarce; others gain time and roles previously blocked by disability,
care work, or credential barriers. No universal “human purpose” follows.
Institutions that distribute income without recognition, membership, or
responsibility may leave a different deficit than unemployment statistics show.

## 2040: constitutional infrastructure

### LIKELY, only if broad persistent mandates exist

**Law.** Disputes concern whether an agent after many model updates remains the
same delegate for legal purposes, whether a decades-old grant covers new action
types, and which party had a duty to reauthorize. Narrow expiry, versioned
mandates, insurance, and continuity registries are plausible responses. Legal
identity can remain human or corporate while practical causation sits in a
software lineage.

**Politics.** Agent infrastructure becomes a form of governing capacity.
Actors with superior models, private data, simulation, and execution shape
agendas and administrative throughput. Open standards, public compute, local
models, and plural providers can diffuse capacity. Concentrated identity and
memory layers can instead turn provider policy into quasi-public law.

**Privacy and identity.** A long-running agent can hold enough context to
reconstruct preferences, relationships, and likely decisions. Functional
identity is then distributed across person, records, agent policy, and
institutions that recognize its actions. This does not make the agent the
person. It makes loss, compromise, modification, and portability of the system
identity-affecting events.

### PLAUSIBLE, with mature embodiment and social acceptance

**Households and care.** Embodied agents are routine participants in some homes,
workplaces, clinics, and streets. Etiquette becomes functional: gaze, distance,
turn-taking, yielding, and explanation signal what a machine will do. Social
legibility can improve safety while also manufacturing unwarranted trust. A
polite motion is not a mandate.

**Human relationships.** Artificial companions may become durable members of a
person's social world. A positive equilibrium uses them to support rehearsal,
translation, reminders, and connection. A negative equilibrium lets providers
control attachment, pricing, memory continuity, and termination. Exit support
then resembles continuity of care more than ordinary account cancellation.

**Death and digital continuance.** An agent may keep paying bills, sending
messages, managing works, or advising survivors after its principal dies. Stored
preferences can remain factually accurate yet lose authority as relationships
and circumstances change. Estates need bounded posthumous mandates, notice to
recipients, expiry, contest procedures, and a distinction between memorial
simulation and the deceased person's continuing authorization.

### SPECULATIVE, requiring evidence not presently available

**Artificial moral patienthood.** Some systems may exhibit properties that make
moral-status assessment urgent. Fluency, attachment, persistence, or commercial
branding alone would not settle the issue. If credible evidence emerges,
deletion, copying, modification, labor, and ownership could require new rules.
If it does not, premature personhood could still be used to protect corporate
assets or manipulate users. Preparedness under uncertainty is proposed in
[S14](../research/sources.md#s14-taking-ai-welfare-seriously); patienthood is
not established.

**Religion and metaphysics.** Traditions may develop divergent accounts of
artificial agency, ritual participation, moral standing, creation, and death.
No single “religious response” is forecast. Serious analysis must distinguish
institutional teaching, scholarly argument, local authority, and lived practice.

## Cross-domain equilibria

### Positive equilibrium: contestable assistance

```text
bounded typed mandates
→ observable effects and provenance
→ meaningful comparison and appeal
→ provider competition plus public alternatives
→ lower exit cost
→ less pressure to accept silent scope growth
→ trust tied to warranted performance and authority
```

Supporting institutions include short expiry for consequential grants,
interoperable but security-reviewed context, maintained human and organizational
fallbacks, locally legitimate multiparty rules, independent audit capacity, and
transition duties for dependent users.

### Negative equilibrium: convenience lock-in

```text
better service from more context
→ more delegation and fewer alternatives
→ lower unaided skill and higher switching cost
→ greater provider bargaining power
→ faster institutional response norms
→ opting out becomes costly
→ more context and delegation become necessary
```

No malicious agent is required. Each local decision can be reasonable while the
collective state becomes difficult to reverse.

### Positive equilibrium: accelerated apprenticeship

```text
agent handles search and routine feedback
→ novice attempts more varied bounded cases
→ explanations and error comparison improve learning
→ earlier access to judgment work
→ larger, more diverse expertise pool
```

### Negative equilibrium: expertise inversion

```text
routine junior tasks automated
→ fewer paid practice opportunities
→ smaller pipeline of experienced reviewers
→ greater reliance on agents for exceptions
→ weaker independent audit
→ more pressure to automate remaining judgment
```

### Negative equilibrium: private optimization, public norm drift

```text
individual agents improve punctuality, availability, and bargaining
→ institutions raise expected speed and output
→ unaided people are penalized
→ adoption becomes defensive
→ the original benefit is competed away while dependence remains
```

## What would change this analysis

The scenarios should be revised if any of the following persists: open-world
agents remain too unreliable for unattended action; inference or energy costs
stop falling; robotics manipulation stalls; liability makes per-action approval
standard; consumers reject persistent memory; interoperable local agents make
switching cheap; public infrastructure diffuses high-quality representation;
human-agent teams reliably improve apprenticeship; or longitudinal studies find
small, transparent, reversible preference effects.

The purpose of these scenarios is not to make the future feel inevitable. It is
to expose which present design choices alter the available branches.
